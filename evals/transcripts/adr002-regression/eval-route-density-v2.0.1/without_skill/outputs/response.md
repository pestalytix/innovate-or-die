You’re probably not missing a better router—you’re missing **route density as a company rule**. Software can sequence a scattered day, but it cannot fix a calendar built from 900 individual customer preferences.

The usual leaks are:

- **Recurring accounts are on arbitrary quarterly due dates.** Re-anchor each account to a geographic “route week” and fixed town/neighborhood day.
- **Sales books by availability, not density.** New customers should be offered “we’re in your area Tuesday/Wednesday,” with exceptions priced or limited.
- **Too many customer-specific windows and reschedules.** For exterior quarterly service, shift toward advance notice + a broad service-day window where your contracts and local requirements allow.
- **Techs cross-cover everywhere.** Give each town/zone a primary tech or crew; use cross-coverage only for capacity or skill exceptions.
- **Callbacks, initials, and add-ons break clean routes.** Hold capacity in each zone-day for them; don’t send a truck across town for a one-off unless it meets a clear economic rule.
- **Your service area has no cost-to-serve boundary.** Outer-area accounts need a designated day, route minimum, or higher price.

At 900 quarterly accounts, I’d do a one-time “route reset,” not another software tweak:

1. Map every account by neighborhood/ZIP and service week.
2. Create small, contiguous zones across the three towns.
3. Assign each zone a primary day (or half-day) and each account a route week.
4. Move customers gradually—“your regular service day is changing to improve consistency”—rather than preserving legacy preferences indefinitely.
5. Track each route weekly: drive hours, stops per drive hour, revenue per route hour, re-services, and out-of-zone exceptions.

The metric I’d put on the wall is **stops per drive hour**, not just total stops. Also make “exception rate” visible: percentage of jobs placed outside their home zone/day. That tells you whether the office is rebuilding the spaghetti every week.

The commercial tradeoff is intentional: offer flexible-date customers a premium option, while standard plans get the route-day model. Many customers care more about reliable communication than picking the weekday.

A practical target: reduce drive time from ~40% first to **30–32%**. That is often achievable through calendar/territory discipline before adding accounts or trucks. Fixed geographic service days and multi-week clustering are the levers route-optimization tools cannot impose on their own.
