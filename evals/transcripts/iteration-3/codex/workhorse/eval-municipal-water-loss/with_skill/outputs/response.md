⟦innovate-or-die v2.1.0⟧

Treat the 22% as an uncertain portfolio—not as proven pipe leakage. With capital locked, the best route is a zero-capex operating-control loop: establish which loss type is material, reduce the duration of verified real losses, and test whether existing operations sustain excess pressure.

Assumption: “no capital” permits staff time, procedural changes, and use of current production, billing, SCADA, pumps, tanks, and valves. It does not mean no operating cost.

## Top opportunities

| Approach | Mechanism | Evidence that confirms it | Evidence that refutes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Time-align and disaggregate the water balance** | Reconcile treatment output, storage change, transfers, billed use, hydrant/flush/construction use, and data uncertainty on the same cutoff dates. Audit zero/flat/estimated/implausible accounts and active-vs-billed services. | In two consecutive balance periods, the same component discrepancy exceeds combined meter/read-timing uncertainty; account samples reveal material billing, meter, bypass, classification, or unrecorded-use defects. | The unexplained balance remains within its uncertainty band, inputs cannot be aligned, or flagged accounts are mostly legitimate occupancy/seasonal variation. Then 22% is not decision-grade evidence of physical loss. |
| **2. Manage “leak-hours avoided,” not jobs closed** | For reported real losses, volume lost is roughly leak rate × time. Timestamp report, isolation, repair, and verification; pre-authorize routine access/traffic/customer-notification delays where permitted; require evidence before closure; review repeat failures and logged overflows/drains. | In an 8-week before/after cohort of at least 25 comparable material events, median report-to-isolation falls ≥30%, repeat calls do not rise, and existing storage-corrected pump/production signals improve for comparable demand periods. | Response time improves but post-repair signals and verified estimates do not; or most losses prove to be hidden background leakage. Keep the process for reliability, but do not claim NRW reduction. |
| **3. Run a reversible, minimum-safe-pressure trial using current controls** | Leakage generally rises with pressure. Use existing pump, tank, or valve schedules to remove excess overnight pressure while preserving service. | In an operationally separable area, compare 14 intervention nights with matched baseline nights, verify no transfer/boundary change, then restore the old setting for 7 nights. Confirm only if pressure falls and normalized overnight flow/pump-volume proxy falls ≥5%, then partially rebounds after restoration—with no fire, customer, residual, turbidity, or critical-customer breach. | Pressure cannot be independently changed, no hydraulic signal changes, or any guardrail is breached. Stop immediately and retain the prior schedule. Check drinking-water, fire-authority, and critical-customer requirements before starting. |

The important reframing: the first approach may reduce *reported* NRW and recover revenue without saving a litre; the latter two can reduce actual treated-water production. Keep those claims separate.

## Secondary no-capex filters

- **Authorized-use control:** require each hydrant, tanker fill, flushing event, municipal use, and contractor withdrawal to be named, time-bounded, and classified as billable, explicitly subsidized, or prohibited. Confirm only if the register explains at least 10% of the statistically material imbalance or unregistered events fall sharply; refute if volumes cannot be estimated or compliance persists.

- **Targeted apparent-loss audit:** sample accounts flagged for zero, flat, estimated, negative, or physically implausible use. Scale only if the 95% lower-confidence estimate shows ≥10% of sampled accounts have material correctable defects *and* conservative annualized recovery is at least twice the added labor/dispute cost. This improves revenue and accounting, not necessarily water saved.

- **Operational-discharge review:** log and challenge tank overflows, drains, flushing, and transient-related discharges. Confirm only with dated records showing safely removable events; refute if they are required for residual, turnover, turbidity control, or fire readiness.

## Most contrarian hypothesis

A large share of the 22% may be timing mismatch, master-meter uncertainty, apparent loss, or recorded-but-unclassified public use—not invisible distribution leakage. Testing this first can prevent three years of misdirected leak activity.

## Cheapest high-information experiment

**Hypothesis:** Existing records can separate an actionable component of the imbalance from measurement/timing uncertainty.

**Critical assumption tested:** Production, storage, billing, and authorized-use data can be aligned closely enough to make a balance meaningful.

**Experiment:** Over six weeks, a production lead and revenue lead create weekly same-cutoff balances for one coherent supply area—or the whole system if that is the only available boundary. Every discrepancy receives an evidence label, owner, and due date.

**Resources:** About 40 staff-hours; existing production/SCADA logs, billing export, work orders, permits, and tank records; no purchases.

**Measured outcome:** Weekly unexplained balance, uncertainty band, and repeatable exceptions with accountable owners.

**Success threshold:** By week 6, at least one component-level discrepancy is outside the uncertainty band in two successive periods and has an executable corrective action.

**Failure threshold:** The imbalance stays within uncertainty, or no material component repeats.

**Learned either way:** Whether the 22% supports targeted operations now or first requires data/measurement assurance.

**If validated:** Direct real-loss components to response-time and pressure trials; handle apparent and authorized-use components through commercial/process controls.

**If falsified:** Correct meter assurance, billing-cycle alignment, and record governance before claiming physical-loss reductions.

## Kill list

- Geographic comparisons without flow/pressure boundaries do not replace DMAs.
- A scorecard or “war room” alone is not a reduction mechanism unless it grants authority and shortens response time.
- Public reporting is a lead source, not evidence of reduced NRW.
- Contractor-term changes depend on current contract and procurement authority.
- Categorizing unmetered public use alone improves accounting; it saves water only if behavior is restricted, rescheduled, or enforced.

Still needed before choosing a sustained program: master-meter accuracy history, SCADA granularity, billing lag, whether pressure is controllable by area, mains-versus-service-line failure mix, and local fire/water-quality/road-permit constraints.
