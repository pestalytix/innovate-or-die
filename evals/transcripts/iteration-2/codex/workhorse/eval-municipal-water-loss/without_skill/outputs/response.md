Treat the 22% as a hypothesis to break into components—not a target by itself. NRW can be real loss (leaks/overflows), apparent loss (metering, billing, theft), or authorized but unbilled use. A repeatable IWA/AWWA-style water balance is the necessary baseline. [EPA water-audit guidance](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P100MEUZ.TXT)

| No-new-capex approach | What to do with existing people/systems | Evidence that confirms it is working | Evidence that refutes or redirects it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reconcile the water balance monthly | Reconcile treatment-plant output, storage change, wholesale transfers, billing exports, adjustments, hydrant flushing, fire use, and known municipal consumption; assign confidence grades. | The unexplained balance narrows; repeat audits agree; a specific component accounts for much of the 22%. | Results vary materially month to month or input-meter/billing uncertainty is larger than the claimed saving—improve data before pursuing field activity. |
| Fix billing and data-handling leakage | Audit account status, meter-read exceptions, estimated bills, zero/negative consumption, multiplier/route errors, vacant accounts, and reversals; correct discovered records. | Corrected accounts produce sustained billed-volume/revenue gains without a matching increase in system input; exception queue declines. | Few material errors, or billed consumption rises only briefly due to catch-up billing. Then apparent loss is not principally a billing-process issue. |
| Target meter investigation, not blanket replacement | Use existing testing bench/portable equipment and field staff to test high-use, old, zero/low-use, and anomalous accounts; clean/repair/re-seat meters from stock where possible. | Tested cohort shows systematic under-registration; post-correction consumption is plausible relative to historical use/peer accounts and persists. | Tests show acceptable accuracy or anomalies disappear when billing records are corrected—do not infer meter error from low usage alone. |
| Enforce and regularize unauthorized use | Match GIS/service records/billing accounts; inspect suspicious active services, construction hydrant use, bypasses, and repeat zero-use premises; use existing enforcement and customer-service processes. | Inspections find unregistered/bypassed consumption; regularized accounts create sustained billed use; unauthorized-use estimate declines. | Low confirmation rate from a documented risk-based sample; do not expand a low-yield patrol program. |
| Accelerate leak response and improve repair quality | Establish a leak-response queue with time-to-isolate and time-to-repair; prioritize visible leaks, customer reports, repeat breaks, and abnormal night-flow locations; verify repairs before closing work orders. This uses operating budget and existing repair inventory, not capital. | Measured flow/pressure-normalized night demand drops after repairs; repaired-leak flows and repair verification show durable reductions; repeat repairs fall. | No measurable post-repair flow reduction, frequent rework, or savings vanish after normalization—investigate isolation errors, repair quality, or wrong leak location. |
| Actively survey the highest-yield areas | Redeploy existing acoustic listening/correlation equipment and trained crews around known break clusters, valves, hydrants, reservoirs, and services; schedule work during quiet/night periods. | Survey finds and confirms leaks at a higher rate or larger estimated flow than reactive work; subsequent minimum-night-flow falls. | Detection yield is low after a defined pilot, or confirmed leaks are too small to affect the balance—redirect crews based on data. |
| Optimize existing pressure-control assets | Review SCADA/field pressure records; correct inappropriate PRV setpoints, valves left in the wrong position, pump schedules, and avoid avoidable high-pressure periods—only within hydraulic, fire-flow, and regulatory limits. | Lower average/excess pressure with maintained service levels; pressure-normalized night flow, breaks, and leak repairs decline. EPA notes that pressure management reduces pressure-sensitive leakage and rupture rates. [EPA](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1009VCZ.TXT) | Customer pressure/quality/fire-flow constraints bind, or flow does not decline after a controlled setpoint change; restore settings and pursue other measures. |
| Stop avoidable overflows and operational waste | Review tank levels, alarms, pump starts, valve lineups, flushing practices, hydrant operations, and contractor water use; meter/estimate and log unavoidable authorized uses. | Overflow/flush logs and tank-level trends show fewer events; authorized-unbilled volume becomes quantified and decreases where operationally safe. | Logs show negligible volume, or reduced flushing compromises water-quality requirements—retain compliant flushing and classify it correctly rather than calling it a leak. |
| Make NRW part of daily operations | Give operations, maintenance, customer service, and finance one weekly exception list and a shared dashboard: system input, billed use, overnight flow, breaks, repair age, meter/billing exceptions, and unauthorized-use cases. | More exceptions reach closure, response time decreases, and rolling 12-month NRW volume—not merely percentage—declines. | Activity counts rise but loss volume does not improve; change prioritization, not just reporting. |

For a utility of this size, I’d run 90-day pilots in the two or three zones or account cohorts most strongly implicated by the audit, using a before/after comparison normalized for demand, weather/season, pressure, and storage changes. Track both:

- NRW volume (e.g., gallons/day), because a percentage can improve merely when consumption rises.
- Recovered revenue, avoided production cost, and staff hours, because apparent and real losses have different value.

The operational priorities align with EPA’s core real-loss controls: pressure management, speed/quality of repairs, and active leakage control; audit results should determine which one deserves effort first. [EPA control guidance](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P1009VCZ.TXT)
