A single SMS at 48 hours is too thin a system. Keep it, but add confirmation, easy rescheduling, and targeted human outreach.

- Send an immediate booking confirmation with a one-tap **Confirm / Reschedule** link.
- Send reminders at **7 days (if booked far ahead), 72–48 hours, and 24 hours**. The last message should ask for an explicit reply—not just provide information.
- Make cancellations frictionless: “Reply C to cancel” or a reschedule link. A late cancellation is far better than an empty chair.
- Call only the highest-risk appointments: prior no-show/late cancel, unconfirmed 24 hours out, new patients, long overdue recare, and high-value blocks. A targeted call list keeps this manageable.
- Use a live short-notice waitlist, segmented by availability, and text it the moment a slot opens.
- At checkout, have the patient verbally repeat the date/time and confirm their preferred mobile number and communication consent.
- Track no-shows by hygienist, day/time, lead time, patient history, and reminder response. Don’t guess: after 30 days, identify the patterns and adjust scheduling/call rules.
- Set a clear, consistently applied late-cancel/no-show policy, while allowing staff discretion for genuine hardship. Require a deposit/card hold only for repeat offenders or long appointments—not as a blanket first move.

A sensible 60-day target is to move from 14% to under 8%, measured separately for confirmed vs. unconfirmed patients. The key metric is not “messages sent,” but **percentage explicitly confirmed at 24 hours** and the resulting show rate.

Text reminders generally improve attendance and perform similarly to phone reminders at lower cost; the operational opportunity is therefore a layered workflow plus selective calls, rather than simply sending more generic texts.
