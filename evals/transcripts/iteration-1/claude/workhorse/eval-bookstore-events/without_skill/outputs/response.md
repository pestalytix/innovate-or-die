This isn't a Claude Code task, so I'll just answer directly.

Before cutting them entirely, I'd separate the two problems: "barely break even" is a math problem, "take a ton of staff time" is an operations problem. Full cancellation solves both by giving up the upside — but events at an indie bookstore usually aren't really about ticket/wine-sale margins, they're customer acquisition and community anchoring that shows up in other numbers (foot traffic, email list growth, halo sales that week). If you're only counting event-night revenue against event-night cost, you might be killing something that's actually working.

A few things I'd check before deciding:
- Do you actually know if event attendees become repeat customers, or is that just an assumption?
- Which events lose money — all of them, or a specific pattern (e.g., debut authors with small draws vs. big-name ones that sell out)?
- Is the pain really "unprofitable" or is it "the same 2 staff people are burned out doing them"?

If the real issue is staff time, a middle path is often better than all-or-nothing: cut frequency (monthly instead of weekly), require publisher co-marketing/costs as a condition of hosting, charge a ticket fee redeemable for the book, or hand off logistics to a paid part-time coordinator/volunteer instead of pulling staff off the floor.
