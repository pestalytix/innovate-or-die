## Context first
14% is within the typical range reported for dental hygiene no-shows (commonly cited benchmarks run ~10–20%), so this isn't an outlier crisis — but a 6-op practice losing ~1 in 7 hygiene slots is meaningful lost chair revenue, and there's real room to move the number down from where you are.

Since you're already doing single-touch 48h SMS, here's what's likely to help further, ranked, with confidence levels stated explicitly.

## High confidence

**1. Add a second, closer-in reminder (day-before evening or morning-of, 2–3 hrs out)**
Single reminders catch people who forgot to write it down; they don't catch people who forgot *again* by the actual day. Multi-touch reminder cadences (e.g., 1 week + 48h + morning-of) consistently outperform single reminders in the literature. This is cheap — most practice-management/comms platforms (Solutionreach, Weave, RevenueWell, Lighthouse 360, etc.) support scheduled multi-step sequences — and it's the highest ROI-per-effort change available.

**2. Make the reminder require an action (confirm or reschedule), not just inform**
A passive "see you Tuesday at 2pm" message is easy to ignore. A message that requires a reply ("Reply C to confirm or R to reschedule") does two things: leverages commitment/follow-through psychology, and — critically — surfaces cancellations 24–48h out instead of 0h out, giving you time to fill the slot from a waitlist. This is a config change on most SMS platforms, not new software.

**3. Target chronic no-show patients specifically**
Prior no-show history is the single strongest predictor of a future no-show — stronger than age, appointment type, or day of week. You don't need a predictive model for this; a simple flag in the PMS ("2+ no-shows in past 12 months") is enough. Route flagged patients to a live phone confirmation call instead of just SMS, and consider requiring a card on file for rebooking after a no-show. Concentrating extra effort on a small high-risk subgroup usually beats blanket policy changes applied to everyone.

## Moderate confidence

**4. Shorten scheduling lead time for hygiene recalls where operationally possible**
No-show risk rises with the gap between booking and appointment date — a 6-month-out recall booked today is meaningfully more likely to be missed than one booked two weeks out. This is harder to act on for hygiene recall specifically (that's the whole point of recall scheduling), but where you have flexibility, prefer shorter-interval booking or add an extra reminder touch scaled to lead time (e.g., an additional check-in message at the 2-week mark for appointments booked >3 months out).

**5. No-show fee / card-on-file for repeat offenders**
Works reasonably well as a deterrent for the chronic-offender segment specifically. Evidence is mixed when applied practice-wide (can increase patient attrition and doesn't move first-time no-shows much), so I'd scope this to patients already flagged under #3 rather than a blanket policy.

**6. Active waitlist with fast-fill process**
Doesn't reduce the no-show rate itself, but converts early cancellations (which #2 will surface more of) into recovered revenue instead of empty chairs. Worth building alongside #2, not instead of it.

## Lower confidence / probably not worth it at this scale

**7. Predictive no-show risk scoring / AI platforms**
In theory the most sophisticated option, but for a single 6-operatory practice you likely don't have the appointment volume for a model to outperform the simple heuristic in #3 (prior no-show history). I'd revisit this only if you're already doing #1–#3 and still stuck.

**8. Overbooking hygiene slots**
This is a revenue-recovery tactic, not a no-show-rate reduction tactic — it hedges against the 14% rather than lowering it, and risks patient wait-time complaints and hygienist overtime if two patients show. I wouldn't lead with this.

**My recommendation on sequencing:** start with #1 and #2 together (likely a settings change in your existing comms platform, days not weeks to implement), layer in #3 for chronic offenders, then reassess the no-show rate after 60–90 days before considering #4–#6.
