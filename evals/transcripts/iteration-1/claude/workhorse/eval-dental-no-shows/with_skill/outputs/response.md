A single 48-hour SMS blast is a weak lever on its own — it's one touchpoint, doesn't require any acknowledgment, and treats every patient the same regardless of their actual show-up risk. 14% is on the high end for hygiene (benchmark is usually 5-10%), so this isn't a "add one more reminder" problem, it's a systems gap. Here's what actually moves the number, roughly in order of impact-to-effort:

**1. Make reminders require a response, not just delivery.**
Switch from a one-way SMS to a confirm/cancel/reschedule flow (most practice-management systems — Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental integrations — support this natively). A reminder that isn't confirmed by 24h out should trigger a phone call from the front desk, not just another text.

**2. Add a second touchpoint at 24h, not just 48h.**
48h is far enough out that it doesn't create urgency — life happens between then and the appointment. Layer in: 1 week (initial), 48h (SMS), 24h (SMS/call), and same-morning (SMS). Hygiene specifically benefits from the tight same-day nudge since it's the easiest appointment type to deprioritize mentally.

**3. Segment by risk, don't treat all patients identically.**
Pull your data: are no-shows concentrated in certain time slots (early AM, post-lunch), certain patient cohorts (new patients, adolescents, Medicaid/self-pay), or certain hygienists? If a small % of patients account for a disproportionate share of no-shows, put a card-on-file/no-show fee policy on repeat offenders specifically rather than practice-wide — it's the highest-friction fix, so reserve it for where it's earned.

**4. Build a same-day hygiene waitlist.**
Six operatories losing 14% of hygiene slots is real revenue leakage even if you eventually fill some of it. A simple "text list" your coordinator can ping when a slot opens (short-notice patients, patients due for recall) recovers a chunk of that loss regardless of whether you ever get no-shows to zero.

**5. Track *why*, not just *that*.**
Have the front desk log a reason code on every no-show/cancellation for 60-90 days (forgot, couldn't reach work, transportation, didn't value the appointment, etc.). That data tells you whether the fix is communication, scheduling friction, or patient education — right now you're guessing.

The highest-leverage single change is usually #1 (confirmation-required reminders) because it converts a passive broadcast into an active loop, and it's a config change, not a new program. I'd start there plus pulling the segmentation data in #3 before adding fees or overbooking, since those are harder to walk back if you get them wrong.
