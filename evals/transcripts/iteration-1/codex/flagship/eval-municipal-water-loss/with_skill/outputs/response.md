## 1. Strongest surviving thesis

The strongest no-capital strategy is a **measure–target–prove operating loop**: validate the 22%, identify its components, redirect existing operating capacity toward the largest verified losses, and credit savings only when independently reconciled.

Fact: NRW includes physical leakage, apparent losses such as meter/billing error or unauthorized consumption, and authorized but unbilled use. A water audit is therefore the starting point, not the diagnosis. [EPA water-loss guidance](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P100MEUZ.TXT), [AWWA water-loss methodology](https://www.awwa.org/resource/water-loss-control/)

“No capital expenditure” does not mean free: every approach below requires an explicit check that staff time, materials, contractor changes, customer service, and legal work fit existing operating authority.

## 2. Most important reframing

Deduction: the utility does not have one 22% problem. It has three separate outcomes to manage:

- **Physical loss:** fewer absolute gallons entering the system or zone after adjusting for demand and storage.
- **Apparent loss:** more consumption accurately authorized, billed, and—separately—collected.
- **Measurement confidence:** narrower uncertainty; valuable, but not water saved.

Use absolute volume and uncertainty alongside the reported percentage. Percentage alone can improve because demand or accounting classifications changed.

## 3. Top opportunities

| Approach | Mechanism | Evidence confirming it | Evidence refuting it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interval-aligned water balance and source-meter challenge** | Reconcile production, storage, transfers, customer-read periods, hydrants, flushing, firefighting, and breaks over matching intervals. Cross-check source meters using existing tank movements, redundant meters and pump operating data. | Repeated, independently corroborated timing or input-measurement bias exceeds twice the combined measurement uncertainty or materially changes the 22% baseline. | Weekly balances already close within stated uncertainty; sensitivity analysis shows plausible corrections would not change priorities. |
| **Premises-to-cash reverse audit** | Start with physical services, parcels, permits, fire services and occupied premises, then prove each maps to a correctly configured account. Examine high-volume meter multipliers, compound meters, bypasses, estimated bills, inactive accounts and data truncation. | A blinded sample finds systematic under-registration or unbilled use; corrections produce sustained additional billed volume with stable system input, and collected revenue exceeds fully loaded investigation cost. | No systematic discrepancy beyond meter/read uncertainty; corrections disappear after subsequent billing cycles or prove uncollectible. |
| **Closed-loop physical-loss control** | Use existing controls for bounded pressure optimization; prioritize repairs by estimated recoverable gallons per crew-hour; require post-repair flow proof; reset pressure after major repairs to prevent rebound leakage. These are extensions of the established real-loss controls of pressure management and faster, higher-quality repair. [World Bank NRW guidance](https://ppp.worldbank.org/water-and-sanitation/performance-based-contracting-tools-non-revenue-water-reduction-tools-resources) | Pressure changes cause repeatable normalized flow changes above measurement uncertainty without service violations; verified gallons recovered per crew-hour improve versus matched historical work. | Response is indistinguishable from demand/storage noise; safety limits intervene; repairs show no attributable flow reduction; or repair capacity is not the actual bottleneck. |

Compact experiment for the first approach:

- **Hypothesis:** measurement or interval errors materially distort the 22%.
- **Experiment:** perform four weekly balances and two independent tank/source-meter comparisons using existing records and equipment.
- **Resources:** planning estimate of 40 staff-hours; $0 capital; authorized O&M only.
- **Success gate:** the same bias appears repeatedly and exceeds both twice the calculated uncertainty and the smallest contemplated intervention signal.
- **Failure gate:** every balance closes within uncertainty and correcting all plausible errors does not change the NRW component ranking.
- **Next step:** if confirmed, correct the baseline before claiming savings; if refuted, move staff directly to component reduction.

Compact experiment for the reverse audit:

- **Hypothesis:** apparent loss is concentrated in identifiable account or meter cohorts.
- **Experiment:** audit a blinded planning sample of 50 accounts—25 high-risk and 25 matched controls—through two billing cycles.
- **Success gate:** sustained corrected and collected value exceeds three times fully loaded audit cost, a proposed local investment threshold.
- **Failure gate:** discrepancies do not exceed combined meter/read uncertainty or collected value fails to cover cost.
- **Next step:** expand only the successful cohort; otherwise stop and redirect resources.

## 4. Most contrarian hypothesis

Hypothesis: existing sewer dry-weather anomalies, recurring potholes, subsidence, persistent dampness, utility-locate records and emergency calls can target field investigation better than a blanket acoustic survey.

Confirm it if blinded, top-ranked locations produce at least twice the confirmed recoverable leakage per field hour of current dispatch. This is a proposed decision gate, not an industry benchmark. Refute it if the advantage disappears out of sample, false positives dominate, or data-access and labor costs exceed the recovered-water value.

## 5. Cheapest high-information experiment

**Hypothesis:** An existing pressure zone contains material pressure-dependent leakage.

**Critical assumption tested:** Legitimate overnight demand and instrument noise are small enough to reveal the pressure response.

**Experiment:** Select one bounded zone with reliable existing inflow and pressure records. Run six comparable nights as three randomized pairs—normal setting versus a lower setting already inside the authorized operating envelope—while adjusting for storage and known night use.

Before testing, copy the applicable numerical limits from local permits, operating procedures and fire-authority requirements into the trial plan: minimum pressure, fire reserve, tank level, disinfectant residual, turbidity, transient limits and critical-customer requirements. Abort at the locally approved alarm buffer.

**Resources:** Planning estimate: 24 staff-hours, six nights, existing SCADA/meters, $0 capital, and no more than $1,500 of already-authorized incremental O&M.

**Measured outcome:** Absolute 1–4 a.m. flow change, combined measurement uncertainty, pressure compliance, storage, water-quality exceptions, complaints and energy use.

**Success threshold:** Repeatable reduction exceeding both 5% of baseline night flow and twice combined measurement uncertainty in at least two of three pairs, with no local limit breached. These are proposed gates to be recalibrated from instrument accuracy and the utility’s marginal value of water.

**Failure threshold:** No replicable response above uncertainty, or any abort limit reached.

**Learned either way:** Whether pressure management can materially reduce leakage in this zone using installed assets.

**If validated:** Repeat in a second zone, annualize verified gallons and O&M/energy effects, then adopt a reversible schedule.

**If falsified:** Restore normal settings and redirect effort to account reconciliation or verified high-flow repairs.

## 6. Compact kill list

- Universal customer-meter replacement: capital-intensive before cohort testing demonstrates material bias.
- Blanket leak surveys: low value when known high-flow repairs already exceed repair capacity.
- Customer conservation as NRW control: reduces legitimate demand, not necessarily loss.
- Public reporting apps or bounties: administrative burden without evidence that reporting is the bottleneck.
- Managing solely to 22%: permits denominator changes and reclassification to masquerade as improvement.

## 7. What may still be missing

Load-bearing assumptions still requiring local verification include source-meter accuracy, zone boundaries, existing sensor quality, repair backlog, O&M headroom, labor agreements, billing and back-billing rules, collectability, warranty or contractor remedies, and state pressure, water-quality and fire-flow requirements.

If the utility already has a queue of independently verified high-flow leaks, conventional rapid repair should win over further experimentation.
