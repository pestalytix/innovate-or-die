The strongest thesis: treat the 45-minute call as a symptom, not the system boundary. Early churn probably combines four different failures: bad-fit sales, onboarding friction, product/reliability failure, and billing that does not match project-paced work. Diagnose the dominant mechanism, then change qualification, delivery architecture, or pricing accordingly.

Assumptions: the product solves a recurring, economically important job; churn can be joined to CRM, usage, support, and billing data; and “logo churn” is not already adjusted for dormant projects.

## Most important reframing

Optimize for a retained economic outcome—not “adoption.” That outcome might be an accepted change-order packet, a clean pay application, preserved notice evidence, or faster collection.

Subcontractors may genuinely be constrained by GC-imposed workflows. It does not follow that they must learn another destination application or pay during months when no suitable project exists.

## Top opportunities

### 1. Retention-underwrite the sale

Before recurring billing starts, require:

- A live qualifying project
- Sufficient event frequency
- The responsible field and office users
- Necessary data access
- One successfully completed real workflow

Vest part of sales compensation on 120-day retained gross profit, not merely contract signature. The radical version is a paid operational-fit assessment, with the company refusing to sell the subscription when recurring value is structurally unlikely.

This is conventional compared with the other ideas, but may be the highest-return move if sales is acquiring firms without active projects, authority, or sufficient workflow volume.

Pilot: Apply the gate to the next 20 eligible opportunities and compare with 20 similar ungated opportunities. Count CAC, implementation labor, lost bookings, COGS, and revenue.

- Advance if 120-day paid retention improves by at least 15 percentage points and contribution per eligible lead does not decline.
- Stop if the lift is below 5 points or contribution per eligible lead falls more than 10%.

### 2. Replace calendar SaaS with project-paced continuity

Charge per active project, project-month, or completed action. Between jobs, retain the archive, integrations, company identity, and perhaps deadline monitoring for free or a nominal fee, with instant paid reactivation.

The mechanism is alignment: customers stop paying for idle capacity without losing accumulated setup and project memory. The radical version is a subcontractor continuity utility—free durable records, paid execution, verification, or transactions.

The danger is merely relabeling churn. Track paid-logo retention, NRR, gross margin, and reactivation separately from total registered logos.

Pilot: Offer a real pause plan to 20 cancellation-intent customers whose stated reason is project timing or cash-flow interruption.

- Advance if at least 50% choose pause and at least 30% resume paid use within six months without material NRR cannibalization.
- Stop if fewer than 25% choose it or fewer than 20% reactivate.

### 3. Make the system operate the workflow

With explicit permission, ingest read-only email, accounting, contract, and field events into a project graph. Automation plus a human review layer returns one useful artifact; customers handle only exceptions.

Start with one trade, one jurisdiction, and one narrow output—for example, a change-order evidence packet or pay-application exception report. This changes the architecture from customer-operated software to managed outcomes. The radical version has no standalone application: the capability lives in email, SMS, accounting software, and existing GC systems.

The main risks are data-access distrust, incorrect project binding, service labor, liability, and contract-specific requirements. Keep consequential legal or contractual outputs as drafts until the appropriate boundaries have been reviewed.

Pilot: Deliver one managed output for 10 target firms at a real price for four weeks.

- Advance if at least four buy, at least 70% of artifacts are accepted without major rework, human labor falls below 30 minutes per active project per week, and projected contribution margin exceeds 50%.
- Stop if fewer than three buy, acceptance is below 50%, or labor remains above an hour per project per week.

## Most contrarian hypothesis

The subcontractor may not be the right payer.

A regional GC could fund preconfigured project rooms because it benefits from fewer rejected submissions and less administrative chasing. Each subcontractor would retain a portable private record and explicitly control what is shared, avoiding a GC-owned surveillance system.

This could turn the product into cross-company acceptance infrastructure, but procurement, trust, data governance, and incumbent construction platforms make it a low-confidence hypothesis.

Test it without building: offer 10 regional GCs a priced, manual pilot for one project.

- Advance if at least two commit budget and at least 50% of invited subcontractors complete a real workflow without vendor handholding.
- Stop if none will fund it or subcontractor participation is below 30%.

A later, even more radical extension is having a licensed financing partner use verified project evidence to improve receivables-financing speed or terms. Do not enter lending before proving that the data changes underwriting.

## Cheapest high-information experiment

**Hypothesis:** At least 40% of early churn is attributable to one dominant mechanism, and its observable markers are at least twice as common among churners as retained peers.

**Critical assumption tested:** Early churn has a dominant, addressable cause rather than many unrelated causes.

**Experiment:** This week, compare 30 customers that churned inside 90 days with 30 retained customers matched by signup period, trade, and size. Predefine observable markers for:

- Bad fit at sale: no suitable project, insufficient event volume, missing authority, or unavailable required data
- Onboarding failure: viable workflow existed, but necessary setup or role handoff was not completed
- Product failure: the customer attempted the workflow but encountered missing capability, reliability, integration, or support failure
- Project/cash intermittency: value was possible or achieved, but work stopped or cash pressure made continuous billing irrational
- Other/unknown

Allow multiple contributing labels, but require an evidence-backed primary cause. Two people should independently code a balanced subset, resolving disagreements before completing the dataset. Collect comparable interview evidence from ten churned and ten retained customers rather than interviewing only churners.

**Resources:** Approximately 16–20 staff hours, existing CRM/product/support/billing data, and up to $400 in interview incentives.

**Measured outcome:** Cause shares, prevalence ratios, coder agreement, unknown rate, and 90-day paid retention by marker.

**Success threshold:** One cause explains at least 40% of churn, is at least twice as prevalent among churners, unknowns are below 20%, and coder agreement exceeds 80%.

**Failure threshold:** No cause reaches 25%, no marker exceeds 1.3× prevalence, unknowns exceed 35%, or agreement is below 65%.

**Learned either way:** Whether to invest in sales gating, onboarding, product reliability, project-paced pricing, or better instrumentation.

**If validated:** Run the corresponding prospective pilot above.

**If falsified:** Instrument promises, project state, first economic outcome, setup progress, and product failures for the next cohort before changing the business model.

## Compact kill list

- Activation bounties: purchase compliance without proving recurring value.
- Generic AI onboarding: automation alone does not change value, incentives, or delivery.
- ROI dashboards: displaying value cannot manufacture it.
- Multi-user onboarding requirements: sensible, but weak if fit or billing is the actual problem.
- Longer contracts or cancellation friction: conceal early failure instead of correcting it.

## What may still be missing

The actual job-to-be-done, ACV, gross margin, trade mix, sales promises, first-value event, and cancellation evidence are unknown. Security rights, contractual notice requirements, and liability boundaries also need verification before implementing data-ingestion, GC-sponsored, or managed-output models.
