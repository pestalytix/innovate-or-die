The fix is to replace the single 48-hour reminder with a **confirmation-and-recovery system**. A reminder that does not require a response still leaves 14% of appointments uncommitted.

For the next 30 days:

- Send a two-way text when the hygiene visit is booked, then a **confirmation-required** text 3–4 days before:  
  “Please reply C to confirm your reserved hygiene visit on Tue, Aug 25 at 10:00 AM, or R to choose another time.”
- At 48 hours, have staff call/text only the patients still unconfirmed. Make rescheduling effortless; a converted cancellation is much easier to refill than a no-show.
- Send a short final reminder the morning before (or 2–3 hours before) to confirmed patients: time, address/parking if useful, and “Reply R if plans changed.”
- Maintain a live short-notice list, segmented by preferred day/time. The instant an opening occurs, offer it by text to 5–10 appropriate patients with a one-tap claim link or a “reply YES” flow.
- Flag repeat behavior: after one no-show, require live confirmation; after two, offer same-day openings or require an appropriate deposit/fee under a clearly communicated, consistently applied policy. Have local counsel/your state dental association review any fee policy.

Front desk language matters: “We’ve reserved this hygiene time specifically for you—does this day and time still work?” That is stronger than “Just reminding you…”

Also find the cause before treating every patient alike. Track every hygiene appointment by:

- provider, day/time, lead time to appointment, patient type (new vs. established), insurance/self-pay, confirmation status, and prior missed visits;
- no-show vs. cancellation with 24-hour notice vs. late cancellation;
- unconfirmed appointments that ultimately attended.

Review this weekly. The first target is to reduce *unconfirmed* hygiene appointments to near zero; then aim to move the 14% no-show rate materially downward over 60–90 days. Do not start blanket double-booking across six operatories—test it only in a specific high-risk slot/provider after you have at least 4–6 weeks of data, because the cost of two patients arriving is real.

Keep messages limited to appointment logistics and honor communication preferences; the ADA notes that appointment reminders are permitted but should disclose only minimal information and accommodate reasonable alternate-contact requests. The ADA also recommends confirmation outreach and calling patients who repeatedly miss visits.
