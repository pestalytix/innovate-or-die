A reported 22% non-revenue water (NRW) is a useful alarm, but not yet a diagnosis: it can reflect real leakage, apparent losses (meter/data/billing errors), or authorized but unbilled use. With capital committed, focus on operational controls, existing data, and maintenance practices.

| No-capex approach | What to do | Evidence it is working / refutes it |
|---|---|---|
| Reconcile the water balance | Audit production, zone/customer-meter totals, billing exports, adjustments, and known authorized uses monthly. Use consistent meter-reading dates. | **Confirm:** discrepancy narrows after correcting identifiable timing, unit, or data errors. **Refute:** independently checked source and customer totals still show a similar gap. |
| Find and correct billing/data errors | Review inactive accounts with consumption, active accounts with zero/very low use, tariff/setup errors, estimated reads, duplicate accounts, and delayed move-in/move-out records. | **Confirm:** corrected accounts increase billed volume/revenue without a change in delivered water. **Refute:** sample audit finds records and bills accurate. |
| Improve use of existing meter data | Flag implausible consumption patterns, prolonged zero reads, sudden step changes, and meter-read exceptions; prioritize field verification using existing staff and equipment. | **Confirm:** field checks reveal stopped, reversed, misread, or incorrectly assigned meters/accounts. **Refute:** sampled exceptions are valid and meter/register reads agree. |
| Enforce repair response and leak triage | Tighten work-order routing, response targets, escalation for visible leaks, and verification that repairs actually stopped flow. Prioritize high-flow and long-running events. | **Confirm:** time from report to isolation/repair falls; repeat calls and estimated run-time decline. **Refute:** repairs are timely but district/night-flow or production demand does not improve. |
| Optimize pressure using existing controls | Review current pressure setpoints, pump schedules, and valve operations; eliminate unnecessarily high pressure while maintaining required customer service and fire-flow obligations. | **Confirm:** lower minimum/night flow and fewer breaks/leak calls, with compliant pressure and no service complaints. **Refute:** pressures cannot safely be reduced, or lowering them does not change flows/break rates. |
| Manage overflow, flushing, and operational losses | Log tank overflows, hydrant flushing, main breaks, drain-downs, treatment-plant wastage, and unauthorized use; reduce avoidable duration/volume through procedure and supervision. | **Confirm:** measured/logged authorized-unbilled and operational-loss volumes decline. **Refute:** records show these volumes are too small to materially affect the balance. |
| Target known chronic-loss areas with existing crews | Use complaint history, break history, wet spots, sewer-inflow reports, pump run-time anomalies, and existing GIS/work orders to schedule inspections and maintenance. | **Confirm:** targeted inspections produce leaks or recurring asset defects at a higher rate than routine work, and repair reduces local demand/complaints. **Refute:** hotspot areas do not yield more findings than background. |
| Control unauthorized consumption | Reconcile hydrant permits, construction draws, municipal uses, and bulk-water records; require existing authorization and logging processes to be followed. | **Confirm:** previously unrecorded use is documented, billed, or classified as authorized-unbilled. **Refute:** field and record checks show little unpermitted use. |
| Improve meter-reading and customer-leak follow-up | Reduce estimates/re-reads; investigate unusually continuous customer use and notify customers promptly. This reduces apparent loss and can reduce real delivered demand. | **Confirm:** estimate rate falls, validated reads rise, and customers confirm/fix downstream leaks. **Refute:** reads are already accurate and flagged accounts show normal use. |
| Establish operational NRW ownership | Assign a cross-functional monthly review—operations, customer service, billing, maintenance—with named actions, owners, and dates. | **Confirm:** actions close on time and the corrected water balance, minimum flows, repair duration, and billed volume improve. **Refute:** the same exceptions recur without ownership or measurable change. |

For a 60,000-connection system, begin by setting a credible baseline: treated-water production, billed authorized consumption, authorized-unbilled use, and apparent versus real-loss estimates. Track both NRW percentage and volume; a percentage alone can improve simply because demand rises. The strongest early evidence is a reconciled monthly water balance paired with operational indicators such as repair duration, overflow volume, estimate-read rate, and—where existing meters permit it—minimum night flow.
